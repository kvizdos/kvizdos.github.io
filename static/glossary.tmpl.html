    Glossary

    Tech runs on acronyms. Here's what the ones I write about actually mean.

    Plain-English definitions of the jargon that shows up across my writing, from web sign-in to EdTech. Every term has its own link, so posts can point straight to it.

    General

    Authentication AuthN
    Proving who you are: a password, a passkey, a code from your phone, or another service vouching for you. It answers "who is this?" and nothing more. Knowing who someone is doesn't say what they're allowed to do; that's authorization. See Authentication vs. Authorization.
    Authorization AuthZ
    Deciding what someone is allowed to do once you know who they are: which records they can see, what they can change, which actions they can take. It always comes after authentication, and it's where most access bugs actually live. See Authentication vs. Authorization.
    SSO Single sign-on
    One login that gets you into many apps, instead of a separate account for each. An identity provider (Google, Microsoft, Okta; in schools, often Clever or ClassLink) vouches for you to each app over OIDC or SAML. It handles authentication, proving who you are; what you're allowed to do there is still each app's authorization.
    OAuth
    A family of protocols for letting one app act on behalf of a user or another server, without handing over passwords. OAuth 1.0a signs every request; OAuth 2.0 hands out tokens, and its client credentials grant is how servers authenticate to each other. (LTI 1.1 uses the former, LTI Advantage the latter.)
    OIDC OpenID Connect
    An identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0 that tells an app who the user is, adding authentication to a protocol built for authorization. It's the protocol behind most "Sign in with…" buttons, and every LTI 1.3 launch.
    JWT JSON Web Token
    A signed bundle of JSON claims: anyone can read it, but only the holder of the key could have produced it. Common as access and ID tokens, and as the launch message in LTI 1.3.
    PKCE Proof Key for Code Exchange
    Pronounced "pixy." An OAuth 2.0 extension that proves whoever redeems a one-time code is the same party that asked for it, so an intercepted code is useless. Built for mobile and browser apps; also handy for safely handing a session from an iframe to a new tab.
    Third-party cookies
    Cookies set by a site embedded inside another site, like an iframe or an ad. Safari blocks them and Firefox partitions them by default, which quietly breaks embedded logins. See how I work around it.
    SFTP SSH File Transfer Protocol
    File transfer over SSH. One system drops files on a server, another picks them up on a schedule. Old, boring, and still how a surprising amount of business data moves around, schools included.

    Schools

    SIS Student Information System
    The school's source of truth for who a student is: demographics, guardians, enrollments, schedules, grades on the transcript, and usually attendance. When two systems disagree, the SIS wins.
    LMS Learning Management System
    Where students actually do the work: courses, assignments, quizzes and the gradebook. Canvas, Schoology, Moodle and Brightspace are common ones. Outside tools usually plug in through LTI.
    LCP Learning Content Provider
    A vendor that sells the course content itself: lessons, modules and activities that schools run inside their LMS.

    EdTech standards

    1EdTech
    The member consortium behind OneRoster, LTI and QTI. Formerly IMS Global Learning Consortium, so older docs use that name.
    OneRoster
    1EdTech's standard for exchanging rosters: students, teachers, classes and enrollments. It comes as CSV files or a REST API, in versions 1.1 and 1.2, and vendors pick which parts they support. "We support OneRoster" always needs a follow-up question. See Rostering.
    Bulk vs. Delta
    The two OneRoster CSV modes. Bulk sends everything, every time; Delta sends only what changed. Many systems only do Bulk, which leaves the receiver to work out what changed.
    LTI Learning Tools Interoperability
    How an LMS launches an outside tool, usually inside an iframe, and tells it who the student is and which course they're in. LTI 1.1 signs requests with OAuth 1.0a and is legacy; LTI 1.3 uses OIDC and signed JWTs. See LTI: Same Spec, Different Dialects.
    LTI Advantage
    Not a version, but a bundle of extra services on top of LTI 1.3: AGS, NRPS and Deep Linking. Its APIs authenticate with the OAuth 2.0 client credentials grant.
    AGS Assignment and Grade Services
    The LTI Advantage service that lets a tool create gradebook columns and send scores back to the LMS.
    NRPS Names and Role Provisioning Services
    The LTI Advantage service that lists who's in the course a tool was launched from. Rostering again, from the same consortium as OneRoster, but scoped to one course.
    Deep Linking
    The LTI Advantage flow that lets a teacher browse a tool's content and drop a specific activity into their course, instead of pasting launch URLs by hand.
    LTI Platform Storage
    A 1EdTech spec that lets a tool in an iframe store launch state through the LMS window, for browsers that block third-party cookies. Only helps if the LMS supports it, and many don't.
    QTI Question & Test Interoperability
    1EdTech's format for moving test questions and assessments between systems.
    Ed-Fi
    A data standard and API from the Ed-Fi Alliance, mostly used to move SIS data into state and district reporting systems.
    SIF Schools Interoperability Framework
    An older K–12 data exchange standard, now run by the Access 4 Learning Community. Many systems still carry it around.
    Clever / ClassLink
    Companies, not standards. They sit between a school's SIS and its vendors to handle rostering and single sign-on, which is often how "standards-based" rostering actually happens.

    Students

    SSID State Student ID
    An ID the state assigns, meant to follow a student between schools in that state. The closest thing to a reliable identifier, when a system actually has it. See The Identity Problem.
    Guardian
    A contact attached to a student in the SIS. That can mean a parent with full rights, a grandparent, an emergency contact, or someone whose rights changed last month. The record rarely tells you which, and once a student becomes an eligible student, the answer can change on their birthday.
    IEP Individualized Education Program
    A legal plan for a student receiving special education services, including accommodations like extended testing time. Vendors often need to know about the accommodations, and rostering standards don't carry them.
    504 plan
    Accommodations for a student with a disability under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, without special education services. Same data problem as an IEP.
    FERPA Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act
    The US federal law protecting student education records. It's why a bad record merge isn't just a bug, it's a privacy incident.
    Eligible student
    Under FERPA, a student who has turned 18 or attends a college or university at any age. Their record rights move from their parents to them. Overnight, a parent who could see everything may no longer be entitled to, even though nothing in the guardian record changed. (Schools may still share with parents of a tax dependent, but that's the school's call.)

    School calendars

    Term
    A fixed block of the school year (semester, trimester, quarter) that classes and enrollments hang off. Grading periods usually sit inside a term.
    Rolling enrollment
    Each student starts and ends a course on their own dates instead of with a term. Common in online schools. Some systems only support terms, others only rolling. See Time Is a Social Construct.
    Instructional hours
    Time a student spends learning, used as "attendance" where seat time doesn't exist, like online and homeschool programs. Often has to be verified by a guardian.
